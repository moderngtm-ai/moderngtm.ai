    <section id="outcome">
      <h2>A plugin that already knows your company</h2>
      <p class="lede">A configured Sales plugin stops asking and starts working your way. Each stock skill lists the company facts it needs and looks for them in your connected tools and instructions. When a fact is missing, the skill asks one question and uses the answer for that conversation only. Configuration writes those answers down once.</p>

      <div class="ledger" role="table" aria-label="Five everyday requests, stock and configured">
        <div class="row head caps" role="row"><span role="columnheader">You ask</span><span role="columnheader">Stock plugin</span><span role="columnheader">Configured plugin</span></div>
        <div class="row" role="row"><div class="ask" role="cell">Triage this inbound lead</div><div class="stock" role="cell">Asks what your ideal customer looks like, then scores against the answer.</div><div class="conf" role="cell">Scores against your written ICP and disqualifiers, and names your handoff channel.</div></div>
        <div class="row" role="row"><div class="ask" role="cell">Prep me for tomorrow's call</div><div class="stock" role="cell">Uses your CRM's stage names, and asks which qualification method you run.</div><div class="conf" role="cell">Builds discovery questions around your qualification method.</div></div>
        <div class="row" role="row"><div class="ask" role="cell">Draft a follow-up</div><div class="stock" role="cell">Writes in your voice if it has learned it, otherwise a neutral one. Knows no company wording rules.</div><div class="conf" role="cell">Follows your messaging rules, approved proof points and signature.</div></div>
        <div class="row" role="row"><div class="ask" role="cell">Write my forecast</div><div class="stock" role="cell">Infers what it can from the CRM, and asks which stages count as commit.</div><div class="conf" role="cell">Uses your mapping of stages to forecast categories.</div></div>
        <div class="row" role="row"><div class="ask" role="cell">Which renewals are coming up?</div><div class="stock" role="cell">Asks where renewal dates live.</div><div class="conf" role="cell">Reads the field you named.</div></div>
      </div>
      <p class="fine">This comparison comes from reading what each stock skill asks for. It is not a benchmark.</p>

      <h3>What it does not do</h3>
      <div class="plain">
        <p>Configuration does not clean a messy CRM or settle a sales process you have not decided. It makes the plugin repeat your decisions, good or bad.</p>
        <p>This guide makes no claims about time saved or deals won. The benefits above are how the plugin is designed to behave. Measure the results on your own team before you quote them.</p>

      <p class="lede">One company configuration serves every role. Roles differ in which skills they use, and so in which facts matter most.</p>
      <p>This map is a suggestion drawn from the skill descriptions. The plugin itself assigns nothing, and no skill is locked to a role.</p>

      <div class="roles">
        <article class="role">
          <h3>SDR or BDR</h3>
          <p class="gets">Inbound leads are scored against your written ICP and disqualifiers, routed by your rules, and handed off in the channel you named with the note you specified.</p>
          <dl>
            <div><dt class="caps">Leans on</dt><dd class="leans">ICP and disqualifiers · Routing and handoff · Value proposition · Wording rules for outreach</dd></div>

            <div class="written"><dt class="caps">Written down, it looks like this</dt><dd>A lead is qualified when a director or above confirms the problem and agrees to a call. Hand off in the inbound channel with company, contact, trigger and next step.</dd></div>

        </article>

      <p class="fine" style="margin-top:14px">The example lines are invented for a fictional company. They show the level of detail that works: one decision, stated plainly.</p>

      <h3>What the stock plugin already does by role</h3>
      <p>The plugin adapts to the person before you configure anything. Its <code>setup</code> skill infers a role from the CRM: AE, BDR or SDR, leader, or customer success. It then builds a role-aware dashboard and recommends three skills to start with. Two skills, <code>team-pipeline</code> and <code>rep-context</code>, are written for leaders.</p>

      <h3>Where configuration differs by role</h3>
      <ul class="list">
        <li><strong>Emphasis.</strong> Every role shares the same facts, but each stresses different ones. Configure first the rows for the roles you actually have.</li>
        <li><strong>Handoffs.</strong> Configuration pays most where work changes hands. Lead to AE needs routing rules and a handoff note. AE to account manager needs renewal dates and products recorded at close.</li>
        <li><strong>The personal layer.</strong> Voice, signature, book and territory belong to one person. Let <code>setup</code> learn them, and keep them in that person's own instructions, not the shared plugin.</li>
        <li><strong>Access.</strong> Owners on Team and Enterprise plans set tool permissions for the whole organization. Claude also acts with each person's own access in the CRM, so your CRM roles still apply.</li>
      </ul>

      <h3>One build or several</h3>
      <p>Keep one build unless two teams truly sell differently. One build means one set of facts to maintain, and the skills already serve every role.</p>
      <p>Separate builds drift apart, and each is another plugin to update. If two segments run different stages or messaging, split by segment, not by job title. Enterprise admins can then set which groups get which plugin.</p>
    </section>

    <section id="seats">
      <h2>Admin, operator, seller: who changes what</h2>
      <p class="lede">Roles decide which skills and facts matter. Seats decide who is allowed to change them. Three people shape a configuration, and each controls a different part of it.</p>
      <p>On a plan of your own you hold all three seats, and nothing here conflicts. On a Team or Enterprise plan the seats are usually different people, so read yours first.</p>

      <div class="seats">
        <article class="seat">
          <header><h3>The admin</h3><p class="pointer">Decide what each tool may do.</p><p>An Owner or Primary Owner of the Claude organization. On Enterprise, also anyone whose custom role manages the organization's libraries. The role labeled Admin in the members list does not open the plugin settings.</p></header>
          <div class="quad">
            <div><span class="caps">You control</span><ul class="list">
              <li>Which connectors the organization has. Each member still signs in with their own account.</li>
              <li>Tool permissions for everyone: always allow, needs approval or blocked. Members cannot loosen them.</li>
              <li>Each plugin's availability: not available, available to install, installed by default or required. On Enterprise, by group.</li>
              <li>What members may do themselves: create or upload plugins, share them, and publish them with or without review.</li>
            </ul></div>
            <div><span class="caps">You do not write</span><ul class="list">
              <li>The sales facts. They belong to the operator.</li>
              <li>Your settings decide who gets the build and what it may do.</li>
            </ul></div>
            <div><span class="caps">Your first move</span><ol class="steps">
              <li>Add the connectors the plugin uses in organization settings. Making a plugin available does not add its connectors.</li>
              <li>Allow reads, put sends, posts and CRM writes on approval, and block what must never run.</li>
              <li>Decide the publishing policy before the operator submits anything.</li>
              <li>When the configured build arrives, set its availability, and decide whether the stock Sales plugin stays visible beside it.</li>
            </ol></div>
            <div><span class="caps">Watch for</span><ul class="list">
              <li>"Installed by default" can be turned off by a member. "Required" cannot.</li>
              <li>A group's setting replaces the organization's for that group, and the most permissive setting wins across several groups.</li>
              <li>Items shared by someone who has left keep working until you stop the sharing.</li>
              <li>In Claude Code, a same-named plugin someone installed themselves loads in place of the copy synced from the organization.</li>
            </ul></div>
          </div>
        </article>

        <article class="seat">
          <header><h3>The operator</h3><p class="pointer">Write the company facts down once.</p><p>Whoever owns the sales process and its tools: revenue operations, sales operations, enablement, or a sales leader or founder doing that job.</p></header>
          <div class="quad">
            <div><span class="caps">You control</span><ul class="list">
              <li>The company facts and skill behavior. This is the build.</li>
              <li>The reference page and the list of open decisions.</li>
              <li>What goes to review, and when.</li>
              <li>The update each time a source document changes.</li>
            </ul></div>
            <div><span class="caps">You depend on</span><ul class="list">
              <li>The admin, for connectors, tool permissions and who receives the plugin.</li>
              <li>Sellers, for how deals are really worked.</li>
              <li>A reviewer's approval for each version, where publishing needs review.</li>
            </ul></div>
            <div><span class="caps">Your first move</span><ol class="steps">
              <li>Gather the source documents and name the open decisions.</li>
              <li>Customize a copy and review it like a contract.</li>
              <li>Try it with a few sellers by sharing it with them directly.</li>
              <li>Publish it to the organization, and agree with the admin who gets it.</li>
            </ol></div>
            <div><span class="caps">Watch for</span><ul class="list">
              <li>Sellers' private copies and instructions drifting from yours. Collect the good parts, then ask them to retire the copies.</li>
              <li>Wording rules that clash with personal voice. Say in the build that company rules win.</li>
              <li>An update that never arrives. A published plugin changes only when you publish it again.</li>
            </ul></div>
          </div>
        </article>

        <article class="seat">
          <header><h3>The seller</h3><p class="pointer">Use the company build, and add your voice.</p><p>An individual contributor using Claude on the company's plan.</p></header>
          <div class="quad">
            <div><span class="caps">You control</span><ul class="list">
              <li>Your sign-in to each tool. Claude acts with your access, so it sees the CRM records you can see.</li>
              <li>Your voice, signature and role, which <code>setup</code> learns.</li>
              <li>Your own project instructions and your scheduled routines.</li>
              <li>Your own plugins, where the organization lets members add them.</li>
            </ul></div>
            <div><span class="caps">You cannot change</span><ul class="list">
              <li>A plugin the organization manages, or one a colleague shared with you.</li>
              <li>A plugin marked required. It stays on.</li>
              <li>Tool permissions set for the organization.</li>
              <li>Which connectors the organization has. You can ask for one where requests are turned on.</li>
            </ul></div>
            <div><span class="caps">Your first move</span><ol class="steps">
              <li>Open Plugins and look for a Sales plugin your organization already provides.</li>
              <li>If there is one, use it. Add only your voice, role and schedules.</li>
              <li>If there is none, start with the stock plugin and put your own facts in project instructions.</li>
              <li>Send what works, and every question a skill still asks, to whoever owns the sales process.</li>
            </ol></div>
            <div><span class="caps">Watch for</span><ul class="list">
              <li>Two Sales plugins turned on. Keep one.</li>
              <li>Company facts in your instructions that disagree with the company build. Delete yours when the build arrives.</li>
              <li>Your own voice in anything you share.</li>
            </ul></div>
          </div>
        </article>
      </div>

      <h3>Two homes for company facts</h3>
      <div class="duo">
        <div class="card"><h4>Project instructions</h4><p>The light route, and the one the stock plugin suggests. Paste your ICP, stages and rules into the instructions of the project where you sell. The stock skills are written to use facts they find there before asking.</p></div>
        <div class="card"><h4>The plugin</h4><p>The durable route. Claude writes the facts into the skills, and you share one package with the team.</p></div>
      </div>
      <p style="margin-top:16px">Start with project instructions if you sell alone and want results today. Move to the plugin when a second person needs the same setup.</p>

      <h3>Use permissions for hard limits</h3>
      <p>Put hard limits in tool permissions, not in skill text. The stock skills are written to follow each connector's settings and to add no restrictions of their own. A blocked tool is therefore the dependable way to stop an action. Skill text is the place for conventions, such as what a call note must contain.</p>

      <h3>What you do not configure here</h3>
      <p>Stage names, field names and picklists are read live from your CRM. Change them in the CRM, and the skills follow.</p>

    <section id="steps">
      <h2>How to configure it, in four phases</h2>
      <p class="lede">Connect your tools, point Claude at your source documents, and ask it to customize the plugin. Claude reads the plugin and your tools, asks about what it cannot find, edits the skills, and hands back a plugin to review and install.</p>
      <p class="fine">Menu names here are as of mid-November 2026, and Anthropic is renaming some of them.</p>

      <div class="phases">
        <div class="phase">
          <h3>Phase 1: Set up</h3>
          <p class="who"><b>Who:</b> each person. On Team and Enterprise plans, an admin enables the connectors first.</p>
          <ol class="steps">
            <li><strong>Install the Sales plugin.</strong> Open "Plugins" in the left sidebar, find Sales and select "Add". Some organizations still see the older sidebar name, "Customize".</li>
            <li><strong>Connect the tools you sell with.</strong> Connect the CRM first, then email, then calendar. That is the order the plugin's <code>setup</code> skill follows. Salesforce and Microsoft 365 need an admin to enable them before anyone can sign in.</li>
            <li><strong>Run setup.</strong> Say "set me up". The skill shows what is connected, infers your role from the CRM and builds a starter dashboard. It also learns your writing voice from sent mail and recommends three skills to start with.</li>
          </ol>
          <p class="fine">Setup saves nothing by itself. It offers a short voice summary for you to keep in your project instructions.</p>
        </div>
        <div class="phase">
          <h3>Phase 2: Configure</h3>
          <p class="who"><b>Who:</b> the operator who owns the sales process. A seller can do it for a copy of their own.</p>
          <ol class="steps">
            <li><strong>Gather your sources of truth.</strong> Collect the documents where sales decisions already live: ICP, messaging, stage definitions, qualification method and brand guide. Put them where Claude can read them, or attach them.</li>
            <li><strong>Ask Claude to customize the plugin.</strong> Open the installed plugin, select "Customize", then "Let's go". Claude reads the plugin and your connected tools, confirms what it found and asks about gaps.</li>

          <h3>Phase 3: Control</h3>
          <p class="who"><b>Who:</b> the admin for permissions. Each person for their own schedules.</p>
          <ol class="steps">
            <li><strong>Set tool permissions.</strong> Each connector tool can be set to "Always allow", "Needs approval" or "Blocked". A sound start is reads allowed, with sends, posts and CRM writes on approval. On Team and Enterprise plans, owners set this for the whole organization.</li>

            <li><strong>Decide what a scheduled run may do.</strong> The plugin's README advises keeping send, post and write tools on approval for scheduled runs. By design, a scheduled run takes only the actions you set it up to take. Anything else it finds becomes a proposal in its output.</li>
          </ol>
        </div>

          <h3>Phase 4: Share and maintain</h3>
          <p class="who"><b>Who:</b> the operator publishes. The admin sets who receives it.</p>
          <ol class="steps">
            <li><strong>Roll it out.</strong> Anthropic's tutorial lists three routes: send the plugin file, host it in a Git repository set up as a marketplace, or have an admin add it for the organization.</li>
            <li><strong>Choose how people get it.</strong> Organization owners set each plugin as available to install, installed by default, required or not available. Enterprise admins can vary that by group.</li>
            <li><strong>Keep it current.</strong> When a source document changes, tell Claude what changed and save the plugin again. A plugin shared directly updates for its recipients. One published to the organization must be published again.</li>
          </ol>

Collect the facts\n\nFor each fact row in scope, in the order from step 0:\n\n1. Look in the sources and the connected tools first.\n2. Keep send, post and write tools on approval for scheduled runs, as the plugin's README advises. By design, a scheduled run takes only the actions it was set up to take. Anything else it finds becomes a proposal in its output.\n4. Have the person choose each task's approval mode on purpose.\n\n**Check:** the chosen routines exist, each with a decided approval mode. Zero routines is a fine answer.\n\n### Step 9. Share and maintain\n\nSkip this step for one person using project instructions.\n\n1. Choose a route to roll it out. Anthropic's tutorial lists three: send the plugin file, host it in a Git repository set up as a marketplace, or have an admin add it for the organization.\n2. Try it with a few sellers first. The operator shares the plugin with specific people, where the organization allows sharing.\n3. Publish it to the organization. The admin sets its availability: not available, available to install, installed by default or required. \"Installed by default\" can be turned off by a member, and \"required\" cannot. Enterprise admins can vary that by group.\n5. The admin adds the plugin's connectors in organization settings. Hiding it avoids two plugins with one name.\n7. Agree how it stays current. When a source document changes, the owner tells Claude what changed and saves the plugin again.
