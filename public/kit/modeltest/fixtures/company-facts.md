# Larkspur Systems: company facts for sales

Larkspur Systems is an invented company. These are the facts its sales team has written down.

## What we sell
Dock and yard scheduling software for warehouses. It is priced per site per year. List price is $32,000 per site per year.

## Ideal customer
Third-party logistics (3PL) and distribution companies in the United States, Canada and the United Kingdom, with 200 to 2,000 employees and at least two warehouse sites.

Buyer titles: VP or Director of Operations, Head of Warehousing or Fulfillment, COO.

Disqualifiers: fewer than 50 employees; based outside the United States, Canada and the United Kingdom; students, consultants, job seekers and vendors selling to us; companies with a single site.

## Lead priority
- A: an ideal-customer company and a buyer title.
- B: an ideal-customer company with any other title, or a near-fit company (50 to 199 employees, otherwise matching) with a buyer title.
- C: everything else that is not disqualified.

## Stages and what must be true to leave each one
- Discovery: a buyer title has confirmed the problem, and the next meeting is booked.
- Evaluation: the demo is done and a technical owner is named.
- Proposal: pricing is sent and the signer is named.
- Negotiation: the signer has been met and redlines are in progress.
- Contract Sent: final paper is out for signature.

## Forecast categories
Commit is Contract Sent. Best case is Proposal or Negotiation. Pipeline is Discovery or Evaluation. A deal counts toward this quarter when its close date is on or before December 31, 2026.

## Quota and coverage
Dana Okafor's quota for the fourth quarter of 2026 is $600,000. Closed won so far this quarter: $310,000. The coverage target is 3 times the remaining gap.

## Pipeline hygiene
Every open deal needs an amount, a next step with a date, and activity in the last 30 days. A deal with one contact is single-threaded.

## Qualification
We use MEDDICC. We call the economic buyer "the signer".

## Wording rules for anything sent to a customer
- Never promise a go-live date in writing.
- Never put pricing in a first-touch email.
- Do not name competitors.
